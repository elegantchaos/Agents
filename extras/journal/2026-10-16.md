## SwiftUI guidance, debugging, and review modes

- In Bookish, every macOS toolbar item flickered whenever a toolbar command button changed availability, and repeatedly at launch. The cause was Commands' `CommandButton`, whose `body` had `if availability != .hidden` as its outermost view; the branch never changed. Finding it took many rounds of builds and manual checks. `performance.md` and `patterns.md` covered conditional structure in general, but `performance.md` had not been read, the `if` was dismissed because its branch never changed, and a reproduction rebuilt from assumptions never contained it. Diff reviews had missed it because `CommandButton` predated the change that first exposed it.
- `swift:swiftui` now has required references (patterns, performance, views, data, api), further references, a Debugging section requiring every reference and an audit of the failing path, including dependencies, before experiments, and a checklist. `performance.md` and `patterns.md` add the specific rule: reusable controls, and toolbar, menu or list-row content, keep a fixed outermost view with any conditional inside a container, with the toolbar flicker as evidence.
- `baseline:standards` gains a Debugging section for every language: read every reference of every relevant skill when tracking down a bug or regression, audit the failing path first, and build reproductions from copies of real code.
- `baseline:review` distinguishes diff and full reviews. A diff review's scope includes code the change newly depends on or places in a new context. After a diff review of a pull request, a full review is offered for each touched package with at most 5,000 source lines, or whose last full review is over 30 days old, predates its last release, or is unrecorded. Full reviews are recorded as journal entries titled "Full Review".
- Baseline bumped to 0.6.0, swift to 0.11.0. The thresholds are Sam's to adjust. Runtime behaviour of the new review modes is untested.
