# Decision Revision Policy

Bookish's decision-log cleanup exposed a conflict between its project rule and the shared records skill: Bookish revised decisions in place, while the skill required a superseding decision by default. Sam chose revision in place as the shared default.

Fetched Agents and checked the current main, unmerged branches, three existing worktrees and the local stash. None contained the intended revision; GitHub returned no open pull requests. The stash has unrelated voice and records edits and was left untouched. The policy fix is isolated on `feature/decision-revision-policy`, based on `origin/main` at `af20cae`.

The records skill now keeps decisions as current intent, retains reasons that prevent reversal, and dates changed direction with a Revised line. Revision in place is the default. Before editing, the agent offers a new superseding decision as the alternative, then obtains a separate confirmation for the chosen edit. A changed premise does not automatically require supersession: it needs Sam's confirmed choice and a concrete reason. Sam's concern is that superseding records add noise and ambiguity over which earlier reasons still apply; one authoritative current account avoids that. An immutable, independently citable approval is a possible use case for supersession, not a requirement established for these projects. Amendments are reserved for rationale that reads more clearly beside the original. The compliance checklist follows the same rule. Both baseline plugin manifests advance to 0.8.33.

Sam also asked to remove the Consequences entry from the shared record shape and any example templates, without replacing it with a negative rule. Essential obligations and applicability limits now sit beside their governing rules, actual trade-offs belong with alternatives, and the checklist checks those responsibilities directly. No separate shared decision example or template containing the section was found. Bookish's log is rewritten separately under Sam's explicit instruction, preserving essential rules and moving design and implementation detail out of the log.
